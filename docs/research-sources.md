# Research sources — Sapiens Metric

Every source used in T-001, with the claim it supports and its limitations.
Only authoritative sources (standards bodies, peer-reviewed/primary
institutional sources, original legal/regulatory sources) are used for
psychometric, scientific, and legal claims.

## S-001 — Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (2014)

- **Source:** American Educational Research Association (AERA), American
  Psychological Association (APA), National Council on Measurement in
  Education (NCME). *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing.*
  Washington, DC: AERA. 2014 edition.
- **URL:** https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html
  (direct PDF: https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf)
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** the definition of validity as the degree to which
  evidence and theory support the interpretations of test scores for proposed
  uses; validity as a property of an interpretation for a specific use;
  distinction between reliability and validity; basis of the claims ladder and
  validation gap analysis in `docs/claims-ladder.md` and
  `docs/validation-norming-gap.md`.
- **Limitations:** U.S.-centric professional standards; not a legal instrument
  and not specific to the Lithuanian/EU context; does not replace local
  regulation or the GDPR.

## S-002 — ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (2nd ed., 2017)

- **Source:** International Test Commission. *The ITC Guidelines for
  Translating and Adapting Tests (Second Edition).* 2017.
- **URL:** https://www.intestcom.org/files/guideline_test_adaptation_2ed.pdf
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** the principle that adapted/localised test versions are
  not simple translations and require their own equivalence evidence; informs
  the language-scope handling in `docs/assessment-principles.md`,
  `docs/item-format-inventory.md`, and OQ-2 in
  `docs/research-open-questions.md`.
- **Limitations:** guidance, not law; adaptation quality must still be
  established empirically per instrument.

## S-003 — General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

- **Source:** Regulation (EU) 2016/679 of the European Parliament and of the
  Council of 27 April 2016 (General Data Protection Regulation). OJ L 119,
  4.5.2016, pp. 1–88.
- **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** the existence of a binding EU data-protection
  framework covering personal data processing, consent, and data-subject
  rights; basis for deferring data-protection/consent/retention/access-control
  decisions to a dedicated legal/privacy review (O-006 in
  `docs/decisions.md`; OQ-7).
- **Limitations:** cited only as the relevant legal framework, not as legal
  advice; no compliance claim is made; detailed application requires
  professional legal review.

## S-004 — CHC theory of cognitive abilities (McGrew, 2009)

- **Source:** McGrew, K. S. (2009). CHC theory and the human cognitive
  abilities project: Standing on the shoulders of the giants of psychometric
  intelligence research. *Intelligence*, 37(1), 1–10.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2008.08.004
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** peer-reviewed source for the Cattell–Horn–Carroll
  (CHC) broad-ability vocabulary that the provisional construct names in
  `docs/measurement-model.md` (fluid reasoning, numerical reasoning,
  spatial/visual reasoning, verbal reasoning, knowledge) are drawn from. Used
  only to ground the provisional construct *vocabulary*, and to support the
  statement that the constructs are merely provisional working hypotheses. It
  is not used to claim that Sapiens Metric itself measures any CHC ability.
- **Limitations:** CHC is a descriptive taxonomy of cognitive abilities, not a
  licence to claim measurement; adopting the vocabulary does not establish
  that Sapiens Metric's tasks validly measure any CHC ability.

## Note on unused sources

No marketing pages, generic blogs, or AI-generated assertions are used as
