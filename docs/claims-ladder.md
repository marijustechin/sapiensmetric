# Claims ladder — Sapiens Metric

Status: draft discovery baseline (T-001). Keyed to the boundaries in
`docs/assessment-principles.md`.

## Purpose

Define, in plain language, what Sapiens Metric may and may not say about its
results at each stage. Claims follow evidence, never precede it. This ladder
governs all product copy, result screens, marketing, and documentation.

## Tier 0 — Permitted now (no validation evidence required)

Safe, true statements about the product itself and about single tasks:

- "This is an exploratory cognitive-ability and knowledge-assessment tool."
- "These are original, rule-based tasks with defined correct answers." — made
  only for items whose provenance has actually been recorded (see O-002 in
  `docs/decisions.md`); not asserted for items lacking recorded provenance.
- "This task reports performance on this specific item type (e.g. number
  series), not general intelligence."
- "Your result is a raw score on the items you completed."
- "Results are pre-validation and not scientifically validated."

Rules for Tier 0:

- Always name the specific task/item type, not an abstract ability.
- Use "score", "performance", or "result" — never "IQ".
- No norm-referenced framing (no "better than X% of people").
- No predictive framing (no "predicts academic/job success").

## Tier 1 — Permitted only with reliability evidence

Statements about consistency/stability of a specific instrument require
reliability evidence (internal consistency, test–retest, etc.) gathered and
reported for that instrument:

- "This test has a reported internal-consistency reliability of …"
- "Scores on this test are stable over a … retest interval (r = …)."

Rules for Tier 1:

- Cite the specific reliability estimate, method, sample, and date.
- Attribute the number to a specific instrument version.

## Tier 2 — Permitted only with validity evidence

Statements about what a score *means* require validity evidence for the
intended interpretation (the degree to which evidence and theory support the
proposed interpretation of scores for the proposed use (S-001)):

- "Scores on this measure are associated with …" (only where demonstrated).
- "This measure is a valid indicator of …" (only where evidence supports the
  interpretation).

Rules for Tier 2:

- Validity is claimed for a specific use, never in the abstract.
- The claim cites the supporting evidence and its source.

## Tier 3 — Permitted only with representative norming

Norm-referenced statements require representative norming data:

- "You scored above X% of a representative sample of …"
- "Your score is at the Nth percentile relative to …"

Rules for Tier 3:

- The norm group, sample size, and representativeness must be stated.
- Percentile/rank claims are impossible without norms and are forbidden
  before they exist.

## Tier 4 — Permitted only after a full validation programme (future; not currently authorised or scoped)

High-stakes / high-consequence claims:

- "IQ score" of any kind — **prohibited** until psychometric validation and
  representative norming exist (and then only under explicit decision).
- Clinical diagnosis — **prohibited**.
- Hiring/selection recommendation — **prohibited** until the instrument is
  validated for that specific organisational use (see O-004 in
  `docs/decisions.md`).
- "Scientifically validated" — **prohibited** until validation and norming
  exist.

## How to use this ladder

- Any new claim must be placed on the ladder and justified by the evidence
  tier it requires.
- If a claim cannot be placed on a tier whose evidence exists, the claim is
  not permitted.
- The ladder is reviewed whenever validation/norming evidence changes.
