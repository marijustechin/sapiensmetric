# Assessment sources — T-015

Source register for T-015 (assessment foundations and first knowledge-pilot
specification). It follows the format of `docs/research-sources.md` and
`docs/t002-research-sources.md`: source, URL/DOI, access date, the claim it
supports, and its limitations. Only authoritative standards-body, primary
institutional, official scientific, or original legal/regulatory sources are
used. No marketing pages, generic blogs, or unsupported AI assertions are used
as evidence.

Access dates: **2026-09-28** unless noted. This register records sources; it does
not resolve any open decision and it makes no validity, norming, legal, or
subject-mastery claim.

## S-001 — Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (2014)

- Already recorded in `docs/research-sources.md` (accessed 2026-09-09); reused.
- **Supports in T-015:** the definition of validity as the degree to which
  evidence and theory support score interpretations for a proposed use;
  reliability vs validity; measurement error/uncertainty; fairness as a
  validation concern; and that score meaning is a property of an interpretation,
  not of a test object. Basis for the "evidence for future ability scores"
  section and the two-tier reporting boundary.
- **Limitations:** U.S.-centric professional standards; guidance, not law; not
  specific to Lithuania/EU or to the GDPR.

## S-002 — ITC Guidelines for Translating and Adapting Tests (2nd ed., 2017)

- Already recorded in `docs/research-sources.md` (accessed 2026-09-09); reused.
- **Supports in T-015:** adapted/localised versions are not simple translations
  and require their own equivalence evidence; basis for the `lt`/`en`
  item-version policy and for the statement that EN/LT equivalence is not
  established by translating an item.
- **Limitations:** guidance, not law; adaptation quality must be established
  empirically per instrument.

## S-003 — General Data Protection Regulation (EU) 2016/679

- Already recorded in `docs/research-sources.md` (accessed 2026-09-09); reused.
- **Supports in T-015:** the existence of a binding EU data-protection framework
  for personal data, consent, and data-subject rights; basis for keeping
  retention/access decisions open (O-006) and for data minimisation in the future
  assessment contract.
- **Limitations:** cited as framework only; not legal advice; no compliance claim.

## S-004 — CHC theory of cognitive abilities (McGrew, 2009)

- Already recorded in `docs/research-sources.md` (accessed 2026-09-09); reused.
- **Supports in T-015:** provisional construct vocabulary only (`C*`/`K*`). It is
  *not* used to claim that any Sapiens Metric task measures a CHC ability.
- **Limitations:** descriptive taxonomy, not a measurement licence.

## S-008 — ITC/ATP Guidelines for Technology-Based Assessment (updated 2025)

- **Source:** International Test Commission (ITC) and Association of Test
  Publishers (ATP). *Guidelines for Technology-Based Assessment.*
- **URL:** https://www.intestcom.org/page/16 (landing page);
  PDF: https://www.intestcom.org/upload/media-library/guidelines-for-technology-based-assessment-v20221108-16684036687NAG8.pdf
  (the ITC page states the document was updated July 2025).
- **Supports in T-015:** that technology-based/online delivery raises
  practice considerations (device/access conditions, item and data security,
  recording of administration conditions, equivalence across delivery modes) that
  belong in a delivery and operations policy. Basis for treating delivery
  conditions, exposure/security, and administration records as explicit design
  concerns rather than afterthoughts.
- **Limitations:** professional guidance, not law; not a validation of any
  Sapiens Metric item or instrument; the 2025 update page was referenced but the
  full document text was not exhaustively reviewed in T-015.

## S-009 — IAU Resolution B5 (2006): Definition of a Planet in the Solar System

- **Source:** International Astronomical Union (IAU), XXVIth General Assembly
  (Prague, 2006), Resolution B5.
- **URL:** https://www.iau.org/static/resolutions/Resolution_GA26-5-6.pdf
  (as reproduced by NASA; see S-013).
- **Supports in T-015:** the three-part definition of a planet (orbits the Sun;
  sufficient mass for hydrostatic-equilibrium/near-round shape; has cleared the
  neighbourhood of its orbit), the definition of a dwarf planet, and the
  "Small Solar System Bodies" category. Basis for the object-classification
  pilot items.
- **Limitations:** an astronomical naming convention, not a claim about item
  difficulty; the definition is debated among planetary scientists (noted on the
  source pages), so items test the adopted IAU definition, not "truth".

## S-010 — NASA Science, "Moon Phases"

- **Source:** National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) Science,
  Earth's Moon — Moon Phases.
- **URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-phases/
  (page content: the eight phases in order; the Moon does not emit its own light;
  the Moon is always half-lit by the Sun; the phase cycle is ~29.5 days; the mean
  Earth–Moon distance is 384,399 km; the Moon orbits once in ~27.322 days; a full
  Moon is opposite the Sun and rises near sunset).
- **Supports in T-015:** moon-phase item content (S-010).
- **Limitations:** descriptive science communication; values are stated as
  rounded/representative; not an education standard and not a psychometric source.

## S-011 — NASA Science, "Solar System Facts"

- **Source:** NASA Science, Solar System Exploration — Solar System Facts.
- **URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/solar-system-facts/
  (page content: eight planets, five officially named dwarf planets, hundreds of
  moons, thousands of asteroids and comets; the four terrestrial planets Mercury,
  Venus, Earth, Mars; gas giants Jupiter and Saturn; ice giants Uranus and
  Neptune; one astronomical unit ≈ 93 million miles / 150 million km; the Milky
  Way as a barred spiral galaxy; the Sun in the Orion Arm/Spur).
- **Supports in T-015:** solar-system structure/scale and object-class items.
- **Limitations:** counts change as discoveries are confirmed; descriptive source,
  not a curriculum standard; rounded values.

## S-012 — NGSS MS-ESS1-1, Earth's Place in the Universe

- **Source:** Next Generation Science Standards (NGSS), performance expectation
  MS-ESS1-1 (and its disciplinary core ideas ESS1.A/ESS1.B).
- **URL:** https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ess1-1-earths-place-universe
  (ESS1.B states that the seasons are a result of Earth's axial tilt and the
  differential intensity of sunlight across the year; the model also explains
  eclipses and lunar phases).
- **Supports in T-015:** use of an identifiable curriculum reference for the
  seasons/eclipses/phases objectives, and the specific "seasons are caused by
  axial tilt and differential sunlight, not by distance" fact used in an item.
- **Limitations:** a U.S. K-12 science standard; it is a reference for
  content/objective framing only. It does **not** establish that a small module
  represents "what every secondary-school graduate worldwide must know", and it
  is not an item-difficulty or psychometric source.

## S-013 — NASA Science, "What is a Planet?"

- **Source:** NASA Science, Planets — What is a Planet?
- **URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/what-is-a-planet/
  (page content: the IAU 2006 three-part definition; the five IAU-recognised
  dwarf planets Ceres, Pluto, Eris, Haumea, Makemake; Pluto's reclassification;
  the ongoing scientific debate about the definition).
- **Supports in T-015:** object-class items and the statement that the IAU
  definition is a convention with an ongoing debate.
- **Limitations:** secondary summary of S-009; science communication, not a
  psychometric or curriculum source.

## S-014 — NASA Science, "Top Moon Questions"

- **Source:** NASA Science, Earth's Moon — Top Moon Questions.
- **URL:** https://science.nasa.gov/moon/top-moon-questions/
  (page content: the Moon is a **natural satellite** and **Earth's** natural
  satellite; it orbits Earth in ~27.3 days; "both Earth and the Moon are moving
  around the Sun"; the Moon does not make its own light — moonlight is reflected
  sunlight).
- **Supports in T-015/T-016:** the corrected AST-A4-004 (moon/satellite vs
  planet) wording and the precise statement that the Earth–Moon system orbits the
  Sun together.
- **Limitations:** science communication, not a psychometric, curriculum, or
  validation source; the definitional exclusion of satellites rests on S-009.

## Note on unused sources

No marketing pages, generic blogs, AI-generated assertions, or unsupported
claims are used as evidence. No legal or compliance conclusion is drawn from any
source. The astronomy sources are used only to support **factual answer keys**
for draft items; they say nothing about item quality, difficulty, reliability, or
validity, none of which exists yet.
