# T-002 research sources — Sapiens Metric

Every authoritative source used in T-002, recorded in the same format as the
T-001 source register (`docs/research-sources.md`): source, URL/DOI, access
date, the claim it supports, and any limitation. Only authoritative scholarly,
standards-body, primary institutional, or original legal/regulatory sources
are used. No marketing pages, generic blogs, or unsupported AI assertions are
used as evidence.

## New sources added in T-002

### S-005 — Berne Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works

- **Source:** World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO). Berne
  Convention for the Protection of Literary and Artistic Works (as amended on
  28 September 1979).
- **URL:** https://www.wipo.int/treaties/en/ip/berne/
  (treaty text: https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/treaties/textdetails/12214)
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** that literary and artistic works receive copyright
  protection on an international basis under an international framework. Used
  only to establish that written material can be subject to copyright and that
  an internal provenance policy is warranted. It is not used to assert that
  any particular test item is a literary work, that every item is
  copyrightable, or that originality is the settled touchstone.
- **Limitations:** a framework treaty; specific protection is determined by
  national law. It does not by itself determine whether a particular item is
  protectable or whether a particular use infringes; those are legal
  conclusions not made here.

### S-006 — Directive 2001/29/EC (InfoSoc Directive)

- **Source:** Directive 2001/29/EC of the European Parliament and of the
  Council of 22 May 2001 on the harmonisation of certain aspects of copyright
  and related rights in the information society. OJ L 167, 22.6.2001,
  pp. 10–19.
- **URL:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32001L0029
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** that EU copyright law establishes an exclusive
  reproduction right for right holders. Used only to justify the conservative
  internal default that third-party material is unusable without a recorded
  permission/licence basis. It is not used to assert the copyright status of
  any particular item or that reproduction always requires authorisation in a
  specific case.
- **Limitations:** cited only as the EU copyright framework, not as legal
  advice; national transposition (including Lithuanian law) applies and no
  compliance conclusion is drawn here.

### S-007 — TRIPS Agreement, Article 9(2)

- **Source:** World Trade Organization. Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of
  Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS), Part II, Section 1, Article 9(2).
- **URL:** https://www.wto.org/english/docs_e/legal_e/27-trips_04_e.htm
- **Access date:** 2026-09-09.
- **Claims supported:** the idea/expression distinction: "Copyright protection
  shall extend to expressions and not to ideas, procedures, methods of
  operation or mathematical concepts as such." Used only to explain why a
  general idea/format family is not automatically off-limits; it is explicitly
  **not** used to provide clearance for any concrete item.
- **Limitations:** an international agreement; the idea/expression line is
  applied case-by-case by national courts. It does not by itself determine
  whether a specific item infringes; that is a legal conclusion not made here.
