# First knowledge pilot — specification (T-015 proposal)

Status: **T-015 proposal for human review**. This is a design specification, not
an approved product decision and not a public test specification. It contains no
validity, difficulty, or norm claim, and it uses **design estimates** only. All
numbers marked *(design estimate)* are uncalibrated targets, not measurements.
The **owner-approved content-selection principle and audience framing** are
recorded in `docs/assessment-foundations.md`; everything else here is a proposal.
Astronomy is a **proposed** first pilot, not an approved subject or the full
product direction. Companion docs: `docs/assessment-foundations.md`,
`docs/pilot-item-samples.md`, `docs/assessment-sources.md`.

## 1. Approved pilot and alternatives

**Owner-approved first pilot (2026-09-28): "Astronomy essentials" — a knowledge
module for adults** (Solar System structure/scale; Earth's motions and seasons;
Moon phases and eclipses; classifying objects). The **adults-only** audience (18+)
is also approved.

Two alternatives considered and not recommended first:

- **Physical geography** — partly stable (mountains, rivers, climate zones) but
  political/administrative geography changes and much is region-specific; sourcing
  and fairness are harder than astronomy.
- **World history** — high interpretive/contested content, periodisation debates,
  and heavy cultural framing; the hardest to source objective answer keys and the
  most likely to embed cultural bias.

Astronomy is recommended because: answer keys rest on **internationally agreed,
authoritative facts** (IAU/NASA; S-009…S-013); the content is **stable over
time** and **low-contest**; it supports **comprehension and application** items,
not only recall; and it keeps clear of proprietary assessment material.

> Owner decisions D-T015-1 (astronomy) and D-T015-2 (adults-only) are **approved**
> (2026-09-28). The **item composition** of the 25-item form remains subject to
> content review before publication; approval of the subject is not approval of the
> draft items.

## 2. Audience and purpose

- **Initial target audience: European**, with **EN (default) / LT** delivery.
- **Broader purpose:** a **defensible foundation of general literacy for
  contemporary global life**, selected under the owner-approved principle —
  "we include a question because understanding its subject helps a person make
  sense of the world—and we can explain why it matters" — covering cultural
  understanding, historical perspective, scientific understanding, and informed
  judgment. **Relevance is not reduced to immediate practical or employment
  utility.**
- **Geography guides presentation and context; it does not restrict whose
  literature, history, or scientific contributions may be included.**
- **Initial-pilot audience:** curious **adults** (18+) taking an **educational
  self-assessment**. Not for children, schools, clinical use, hiring, or
  qualification.
- **Adults-only scope (owner-approved, D-T015-2)** — avoids child-data governance
  and age-appropriateness questions while O-006 is open.
- **Not claimed:** a universal curriculum, a population-wide knowledge standard,
  or secondary-school qualification equivalence.

## 3. Language and cultural assumptions

- **English is the default**; **Lithuanian** is supported. Prompts and options are
  authored in each language as **distinct, versioned items**, not free-text
  translations (D-007; S-002). Instruction text is localised.
- **No EN/LT equivalence is claimed.** The item sample is not validated in either
  language, and drafting in two languages does **not** create equivalence.
- **Geography guides presentation, not inclusion:** the European audience affects
  context and examples, not whose contributions are eligible. (For this astronomy
  pilot the point is latent, but it governs later literature/history subjects.)
- Content uses **internationally agreed astronomy** and avoids region-specific
  trivia. Where a phenomenon is **hemisphere/latitude dependent** (e.g. moonrise
  timing, seasonal wording), items avoid it or phrase it to be location-neutral;
  the seasons item attributes cause to axial tilt (S-012).
- Units: prefer **kilometres/AU** with a plain-language comparator; avoid
  metric/imperial ambiguity. No local calendar/cultural content.

## 4. Learning objectives and exclusions

**Objectives** (pilot scope):

- **A1 — Solar System structure and scale.** Identify the Sun, the eight planets
  and their order, terrestrial vs giant planets, dwarf planets, moons, asteroids
  and comets; the astronomical unit (AU) as Earth–Sun distance (S-011).
  *Why this matters:* a basic mental map of the planetary system is the frame for
  understanding space science, seasons, eclipses, and cosmic scale — it turns
  scattered facts into a coherent picture of where we are.
- **A2 — Earth's motions and seasons.** Distinguish rotation (day) from
  revolution (year); explain that seasons are caused by axial tilt and the
  differential intensity of sunlight, **not** by distance from the Sun (S-012).
  *Why this matters:* the day/night/year cycle and the seasons shape daily life,
  calendars, agriculture, and climate discussion, and the tilt corrects a very
  common misconception.
- **A3 — Moon phases and eclipses.** Why phases occur (the Moon is always
  half-lit; we see varying fractions of the illuminated half); the order of the
  eight phases; that a full Moon is opposite the Sun; that solar eclipse requires
  the Moon between Earth and Sun (new-Moon alignment) (S-010, S-012).
  *Why this matters:* the Moon's cycle is one of the oldest human time-keeping and
  cultural references and a shared, observable entry point to celestial geometry.
- **A4 — Object classification.** Apply the adopted IAU definition: planet vs
  dwarf planet vs small Solar System body vs moon/star (S-009, S-013).
  *Why this matters:* classification shows how science draws boundaries from
  evidence (orbit, shape, neighbourhood) and why categories change with discovery
  — the visible case being Pluto and the dwarf planets.
- **A5 — Scale and light-time application.** Apply the AU and the finite speed of
  light to simple reasoning about distance/travel time (S-010, S-011).
  *Why this matters:* grasping astronomical scale — and that looking out is
  looking back in time — is central to scientific literacy and to reading
  astronomy news.

**Exclusions:** astrology; space weather and current events; mission/launch
trivia that dates quickly; detailed physics (relativity, orbital mechanics
maths); exoplanet specifics; the scientific *debate* about the planet definition
(scored only as the adopted IAU convention, not as "truth"); any protected
assessment content.

## 5. Content blueprint (objectives → item types and coverage)

*(Design estimate; not calibrated. Item counts are targets for a first form.)*

| Objective | Item types (proposed) | Items *(estimate)* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1 Structure & scale | ordering; single-answer | 5 |
| A2 Motions & seasons | single-answer ("best explains"); comprehension | 5 |
| A3 Phases & eclipses | single-answer; ordering; application | 5 |
| A4 Classification | single-answer; multiple-select (apply criteria) | 5 |
| A5 Scale & light-time | short numeric/application | 5 |
| **Total** | | **25** |

**Five items per objective; 5 × 5 = 25** items per form *(design estimate)*.
**Owner-approved:** a **25-item form** (5 per objective) as the proposed form;
the **final item composition remains subject to content review**, and approval of
the form does not approve the draft items. At least one item per objective must be
**more than factual recall** (comprehension or application), and no objective may
be assessed by recall alone. The recalled foundational content (e.g. planet order)
is retained only where it is a prerequisite for reasoning about the subject.

## 6. Item count and duration *(design estimates)*

- **Items:** **25** per form (design estimate; 5 per objective).
- **Expected duration:** **~20–28 minutes** (design estimate; a median of roughly
  50–60 seconds per item for a general-adult reader). This is an **estimate**, not
  a measured administration time.

## 7. Timing position

**Untimed (power) delivery [Proposal].** Timing is **not** part of the score and
there is **no time limit that changes the score**. Rationale: speed measurement
(`S1`) is deferred in `docs/measurement-model.md`; timing introduces
device/connectivity/fairness confounds we cannot yet control. A soft "suggested
session length" may be shown as guidance only, and it must not be scored.

## 8. Scoring, skips, guessing, completion, interruption

**[Proposal]** (raw, transparent scoring; Tier 0):

- **Single-answer:** 1 point if correct, else 0.
- **Multiple-select:** 1 point only if the selected set **exactly** equals the
  key (all-or-nothing); **no partial credit** in the pilot (partial weighting is
  arbitrary without calibration).
- **Ordering:** 1 point only if the **full order** is correct.
- **Numeric/application:** 1 point if within the stated accepted tolerance.
- **Finalised total (only on explicit submission):** sum of item points over the
  **full form denominator (25)**. Unanswered items in a submitted attempt are
  **skipped** and receive **0**; the number skipped is **reported separately**. The
  denominator does **not** shrink to the answered set.
- **Unfinished attempts (not submitted):** show **progress only** (e.g. "17 of 25
  answered — not submitted") with an **incomplete** status. They are **not**
  assigned a final comparable score and are **not** scored over the answered set
  (that would reward selective completion). They are excluded from comparable
  history statistics.
- **Skipped vs unanswered:** "skipped" means an item was passed over in a
  **submitted** attempt (0 points, counted separately); an unfinished attempt has
  "unanswered" items and receives no final score.
- **Guessing:** **no guessing penalty and no correction** in the pilot. Raw
  scoring is used; the number of options per item is visible, but no "above
  chance" inference is drawn (`docs/claims-ladder.md`).
- **Completion / interruption / resumption:** lifecycle
  `started` → `submitted` | `abandoned`. An in-progress attempt may be
  **resumed within the same session** with the same form and item versions, and
  then explicitly submitted. Abandoning (or an expired session) marks the attempt
  **incomplete**; it is never presented as a completed comparable result.

## 9. Feedback and exposure policy

**[Proposal]** — one coherent policy (full rationale and trade-offs in
`docs/assessment-foundations.md` §4). It resolves the tension between giving
useful explanations and keeping the assessment bank fresh: it cannot do both
perfectly, so it chooses educational feedback for seen items and manages integrity
explicitly.

- **Shown, and when.** During an assessment attempt: no answers or correctness
  signals. After **explicit final submission:** per-item correctness, a short
  explanation, and the source for the items the participant actually saw, plus the
  objective-level summary. Practice items (a separate, non-overlapping pool) give
  immediate explanations.
- **Exposure.** Every presented item records an exposure event (participant,
  attempt, date, order). After submission, seen assessment items are marked
  **exposed to this participant** and are **not re-served while fresh items
  remain**.
- **Insufficient unseen items — learning repeat.** Use previously seen items in a
  clearly labelled **learning repeat**, explicitly distinct from a fresh
  assessment: the result is more practised and **not comparable** to a first
  attempt, and a score increase must **not** be presented as independent
  improvement. Growing the bank via the item-review pipeline is the real fix.
- **Honest limits.** This does **not** keep the bank unexposed, does **not** claim
  random forms are equally difficult, and a repeat score is **not** a fresh
  equivalent measurement. Registration cannot prevent multiple accounts, outside
  help, or AI use.
- **What it means for the learner.** They always get a useful explanation of the
  items they saw; repeats are framed as practice, not as a new comparable result.

## 10. What can be reported immediately vs later evidence

**Immediately (Tier 0, honest now):**

- Total **raw score** (e.g. "17 of 25 correct").
- **Per-objective counts** where the objective has enough items (see §11).
- Number **skipped**; completed/incomplete and **first/repeat** labels.
- Per-item correct/incorrect with explanations and sources.
- An explicit **pre-validation** statement of what the result does and does not
  mean.

**Only with later evidence (not now):**

- Item **difficulty/discrimination**, distractor functioning.
- **Reliability** (Tier 1) and **validity** for a stated use (Tier 2).
- **Norms/percentiles** (Tier 3) and any **ability/IQ** interpretation (Tier 4).
- EN/LT **equivalence**.

## 11. When there are too few items for a topic breakdown

If an objective has **fewer than four items** on a form, do **not** report a
percentage or a "topic strength/weakness" statement for it. Report only the total
score and mark that objective **"not reported"** (or show the raw "X of Y"
without any qualitative label). Rationale: with very few items, an objective
count is dominated by item-specific quirks and chance; presenting it as a topic
result would over-read noise. Even at the proposed 4–5 items per objective, the
breakdown is **descriptive only**, never a mastery claim.

## 12. Proposed user journey (text only; not implemented)

1. **Register / log in** (registration is required to take an assessment). English
   default; locale switch available.
2. **Choose "Astronomy essentials"** (a knowledge module, separate from any
   reasoning/ability content). The module intro states coverage, the ~20–25
   minute estimate, and the honest boundary wording.
3. **Read instructions:** untimed; no guessing penalty; one correct set for
   multiple-select; answers are not shown during the assessment; you may skip.
4. **Take the 25 items;** progress is shown; the attempt can be paused/resumed
   within the session.
5. **Submit** (explicitly). Only then does the server produce a final score over
   the full form, using the versioned scoring rule. Leaving without submitting
   shows progress and an **incomplete** status, with no comparable score.
6. **Result page:** total raw score, per-objective counts, skipped count,
   first/repeat label, item-by-item review with explanations/sources, and a
   concise pre-validation statement.
7. **Private history / statistics:** past attempts, with an explicit note that
   scores across attempts/forms/versions are **not** directly comparable without
   equating evidence, and that a higher repeat score may reflect learning,
   familiarity, exposure, or assistance. History is **descriptive**: it lists past
   attempts and does **not** measure progress or demonstrate improvement.

### Example result wording (proposed; Tier 0)

> **Astronomy essentials — first attempt**
> **You answered 17 of 25 items correctly.**
>
> What to take from this:
> - In this attempt, you answered the most items correctly in Solar System
>   structure and scale (4 of 5) and Moon phases and eclipses (4 of 5).
> - You answered fewer items correctly in Earth's motions and seasons (3 of 5),
>   object classification (3 of 5), and scale and light-time (3 of 5).
> - Skipped: 2 — these count as not correct and are shown separately.
>
> These are counts from one sitting; a one-item difference in a five-item section
> is not a stable strength or weakness.
>
> The explanations below show why each answer is what it is; that is the main
> point of the module.
>
> About this result: it describes your performance on this module's specific,
> versioned items. Objective denominators total 25 (5 per objective), of which 17
> were answered correctly. It is **pre-validation** — not an IQ score, not a
> percentile, not a mastery grade, and not a validated measure of general
> knowledge or ability. It is not comparable with other people, with other
> modules, or with the app's other content. Your saved history lists past attempts
> descriptively; it does not measure progress.

## 14. Non-claims of this specification

- No pass threshold, percentile, IQ conversion, "above average" label, mastery
  claim, or population ranking is defined (and none may be added before the
  corresponding evidence tier exists).
- **No universal curriculum, no population-wide knowledge standard, and no
  secondary-school qualification equivalence** are claimed.
- No **validated general-literacy** claim: the module reports performance on its
  specific versioned items only.
- A small astronomy module is a bounded content sample tied to identified
  objectives and sources; it does not represent "what every secondary-school
  graduate worldwide must know".
- **Astronomy remains a *proposed* first pilot**, not an approved subject and not
  the full product direction. All methodological choices here are proposals unless
  explicitly approved.
